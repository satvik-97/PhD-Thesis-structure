\chapter{Conclusions and Future Work}
\label{ch:conclusion}

\section{The common thread}
\label{sec:conclusion_thread}

Every chapter of this thesis approaches the same problem from a different angle. Compressing a cosmological or astrophysical field down to a handful of summary statistics before doing inference on it throws away information, and increasingly that discarded information is exactly what upcoming, high resolution surveys are sensitive to (Section~\ref{sec:lss_param_inference}). The response, throughout, has been to work with the field itself, or a learned representation of it, using generative models trained directly on hydrodynamical simulations rather than on hand calibrated prescriptions or perturbative expansions that break down on the scales of interest.

What is worth stating explicitly here is that this is not five independent applications of the same idea. The chapters build on one another methodologically.. \LODI\ (Chapter~\ref{ch:lodi}) first demonstrates that a two stage, conditional generative pipeline can paint a full field onto a dark matter only input, and uses a latent overlap scheme, adapted from inpainting, to stitch small trained sub-volumes into a coherent large one. \FOLD\ (Chapter~\ref{ch:cosmofold}) exists specifically because that overlap scheme has a real cost (a $729/512\approx 1.4\times$ inflation in the number of sub-volumes needed, by \LODI's own accounting) and a real failure mode on extended structures; its sliding window generation removes the explicit overlap prescription entirely by folding the coherence requirement into the diffusion trajectory itself. And as discussed many times over, non Gaussianity needs to be captured at intermediate scales, for which the chapter introduces positional encoding to further improve capturing of higher order correlations. Chapter~\ref{ch:field_pos} then takes that same architecture and asks a different question of it. Not just a point estimate, but a posterior, with an external cosmological prior (Planck) injected through Bayes' theorem at inference time, without retraining. Chapter~\ref{ch:dps} generalizes this last move into a full framework, diffusion posterior sampling, derived from first principles rather than asserted, and applies it to a genuinely different inverse problem, Lyman-$\alpha$ tomography, where the forward model is not another neural network but a differentiable physical one (the Voigt-profile optical depth calculation). Chapter~\ref{ch:emulator} closes the loop by asking what all of this is \emph{for}: it quantifies, in the currency cosmologists actually care about (a figure of merit on $\Om$ and $\sige$), how much is gained by moving from a power spectrum to a full 3D field, and from one tracer to two.

Read this way, the thesis has a clear arc. Can a generative model paint a field (Chapter~\ref{ch:lodi})? Can it do so coherently and efficiently at scale (Chapter~\ref{ch:cosmofold})? Can it produce not just a point estimate but a field posterior, and accept external information without retraining (Chapter~\ref{ch:field_pos})? Can that posterior sampling machinery be formalized and pointed at a real observational inverse problem with a physical forward model (Chapter~\ref{ch:dps})? And, finally, does any of this actually buy back cosmological constraining power (Chapter~\ref{ch:emulator})? The answer to the last question is the clearest number in the thesis: moving from a power spectrum to a full 3D field representation increases the median figure of merit on $(\Om,\sige)$ by roughly a factor of 3, and combining two tracers rather than one buys a further factor of 2-7 depending on the case (Section~\ref{sec:emu_downstream}), while every relative bias on the recovered parameters stays below about 1.5\%. That is the payoff the rest of the thesis is building the machinery to obtain.

\section{What has been demonstrated}
\label{sec:conclusion_recap}

This section recaps, briefly and chapter by chapter, only the results each chapter itself already establishes; the point of restating them here is the synthesis in Section~\ref{sec:conclusion_thread} above and the cross-cutting discussion below, not repetition for its own sake.

\LODI\ (Chapter~\ref{ch:lodi}) showed that a conditional U-Net plus a conditional variational diffusion model, painting the 21\,cm brightness temperature field onto a predicted halo distribution, recovers the 21\,cm power spectrum to within 10\% on average up to $k\approx10\hMpci$, and that the latent-overlap generation scheme gives roughly a $6\times$ speed-up over naive sequential generation, at a full-pipeline cost of about 106 seconds per generated $256^3$ volume on a single A100. \FOLD\ (Chapter~\ref{ch:cosmofold}) showed that removing the explicit overlap prescription in favor of a sliding-window trajectory both fixes \LODI's failure on extended structure and reduces generation time by roughly 40\% for a comparable case, while a model trained only on $25\Mpch$ CAMELS boxes transfers to the $205\Mpch$ TNG volume with the power spectrum accurate to within 10\% up to $k\lesssim5\hMpci$, a genuine box-size generalization result, not just a within distribution one. The Chapter also shows that positional encoding further improves multi scale Bispectrum results which otherwise are lost at inference, especially with a sparse conditioning map. Chapter~\ref{ch:field_pos} showed that the same architecture can be turned into a posterior sampler, trained across the CAMELS Latin Hypercube so that astrophysical feedback is marginalized over implicitly, and that an external Planck posterior on $(\Om,\sige)$ can be folded into the generative process at inference time with no retraining. The chapter's own discussion is explicit that its demonstration rests on a single test field and that a fuller calibration study is still needed. Chapter~\ref{ch:dps} showed that a single, unconditionally trained diffusion prior over a joint five (4 baryonic and 1 dark matter), combined with a differentiable forward physics model, recovers the Lyman-$\alpha$ forest tomography fields' closely down to the non-linear scales, across a range of sightline densities and noise levels, without ever retraining between cases, and, most notably, that the dark matter field, which never enters the forward model at all, is still recovered via correlations the prior learned purely from unconditional training. Chapter~\ref{ch:emulator} showed that the same generative machinery, generalized across cosmological and astrophysical parameters via FiLM conditioning, generates a galaxy/HI field pair in about $10^{-2}$ seconds on a GPU, and that using it as the forward simulator inside a full 3D field-level SBI pipeline recovers $\Om$ and $\sige$ with sub percent level bias and several fold tighter constraints than an equivalent power spectrum analysis.

\section{Cross-cutting discussion}
\label{sec:conclusion_discussion}

\subsection{On data: how much, and of what kind}
\label{sec:conclusion_disc_data}

A pattern recurs across every chapter. Every model in this thesis is trained on a strikingly small number of independent simulations, made usable only through aggressive sub-volume chunking and augmentation. \LODI\ trains its diffusion model on 5 simulations; \FOLD's TNG-direct case uses 4; Chapter~\ref{ch:field_pos} uses 700 (plus 300 for testing) from the CAMELS Latin Hypercube; Chapter~\ref{ch:dps} uses 8 (plus 2 for testing); Chapter~\ref{ch:emulator}'s emulator is trained on 508 seeds, and uses $24\times$ rotational augmentation to increase the perceived dataset. In every case where an out of distribution test was actually attempted, some degradation was found: \FOLD's power spectrum visibly degrades beyond the training box's own largest mode when applied to a $8\times$ larger volume; Chapter~\ref{ch:emulator}'s emulator likewise shows a "visibly less accurate" power spectrum at $50\Mpch$ after training only at $25\Mpch$, and it has been discussed that the pipeline has never been tested against an independently generated simulation suite at all, citing the concern (via \citealt{Bayer_2025}) that even small inter-code numerical differences can bias an inferred posterior. Put plainly, this thesis has repeatedly shown that these models interpolate well within the distribution they were shown, and has repeatedly flagged, without yet resolving, the question of what happens outside it. This is precisely the gap that Future Work item~\ref{sec:conclusion_future_jepa} below is aimed at.

\subsection{On computational cost and time}
\label{sec:conclusion_disc_compute}

The motivation stated in the Introduction (Section~\ref{sec:sim_bottleneck}) is quantitative: the IllustrisTNG simulations cost on the order of $35$ million and $18$ million CPU-hours for their two largest runs, and EAGLE cost roughly $4.5$ million. It is worth collecting, in one place, just how large a gap this thesis's models open up against that baseline, since each chapter only ever quotes its own number. \LODI's full two-stage pipeline generates one $256^3$ volume in about 106 seconds on a single GPU (with 50 diffusion time steps), after roughly 50 GPU-hours of one-time training. \FOLD, by reducing the working resolution, cuts generation time, bringing a full volume generation of TNG-300 down to about 4 hours (See Table~\ref{tab:fields} for more details). While Chapter~\ref{ch:emulator}'s emulator, once trained, generates a galaxy/HI field pair in about $10^{-2}$ seconds. These are not incremental speed-ups over the simulations they stand in for. They are the difference between a computation that requires a dedicated allocation on a supercomputing facility and one that can be run, repeatedly, inside the inner loop of a simulation-based inference pipeline, which is precisely why Chapter~\ref{ch:emulator}'s SBI results (hundreds of thousands of forward simulations) are possible at all. The cost of this speed up, as Section~\ref{sec:conclusion_disc_data} makes clear, is that it is bought against a training set that is small relative to the parameter space these methods are ultimately meant to cover. Ofcourse it is important that it is made clear that when doing one to one fidelity comparison between simulated and emulated fields, we do accept that with increased generation/emulation speed, there is a loss of accuracy, especially in the smallest scales, highest densities and the highest order correlations.

\subsection{Philosophy: what a learned prior actually knows}
\label{sec:conclusion_disc_philosophy}

As stated multiple times across this manuscript, every method here is bounded above by the fidelity of the simulations it is trained on, and no amount of architectural cleverness changes that. What this thesis adds to that observation is an informative empirical probe of \emph{how much} of a simulation's physics a learned prior actually internalizes, rather than merely reproduces conditionally. Chapter~\ref{ch:dps}'s dark matter recovery is the cleanest version of this: because $\dmLOS$ never enters the likelihood, its reconstruction is not a statement about the forward model at all, it is a direct, quantitative readout of whatever correlation between dark matter and the gas phase tracers the prior absorbed during unconditional training. That correlation is not hand specified anywhere in the pipeline. It is exactly the kind of implicit, statistically learned physical knowledge that a purely forward model based method has no mechanism to produce, because it never has the opportunity to learn it.

This cuts both ways, and Chapter~\ref{ch:emulator}'s own discussion surfaces the other side of it. The checkpoint that minimizes voxel wise reconstruction loss is not the same checkpoint that best reproduces the power spectrum. Neither loss is wrong, but neither is a complete stand in for "is this model's implicit physical knowledge trustworthy for the downstream scientific question." Across this thesis, no single validation metric, not a pixel loss, not a power spectrum residual, not a posterior correlation coefficient, fully answers that question on its own. Each chapter's validation is really several partial, complementary answers to it (which is itself a useful, if implicit, methodological lesson going forward). The honest position, and the one this thesis is implicitly built around, is that a learned generative prior over a cosmological field is best understood not as a black-
 box interpolator but as a compressed, implicit, and only partially interpretable encoding of the structure formation physics present in its training simulations, no more faithful than the physics those simulations themselves capture, and no more general than the range of scales, tracers, and conditions those simulations spanned.

\subsection{Limitations and caveats, thesis-wide}
\label{sec:conclusion_disc_limits}

Beyond the per chapter limitations already stated (\LODI's untested cosmology/redshift generalization; Chapter~\ref{ch:emulator}'s box-size and out of distribution gaps), three limitations are visible only by looking across chapters:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Astrophysics marginalization is inconsistent across chapters.} Chapter~\ref{ch:field_pos} and Chapter~\ref{ch:emulator} both train across a range of feedback parameters (the CAMELS Latin Hypercube), so their generative priors already marginalize over astrophysical uncertainty by construction. \LODI, \FOLD, and Chapter~\ref{ch:dps} do not: all three are trained at a single, fixed astrophysical model (and, for Chapter~\ref{ch:dps}, a single fixed UV background and thermal history). This is not a flaw in any one chapter, each choice was deliberate, but it means the thesis's own posterior sampling and inverse problem machinery (Chapter~\ref{ch:dps}) has not yet been combined with its own demonstrated ability to marginalize over feedback uncertainty (Chapter~\ref{ch:field_pos}, Chapter~\ref{ch:emulator}). Doing so is a natural extension and is proposed explicitly below (Section~\ref{sec:conclusion_future_astro}).
    \item \textbf{Everything is validated against simulation truth, not survey-realistic data.} No chapter yet runs its full pipeline against data carrying realistic instrumental noise, beam effects, masking, or a survey selection function end to end; Chapter~\ref{ch:dps} varies signal to noise and sightline coverage synthetically, and Chapter~\ref{ch:emulator}'s discussion names this explicitly as future work, but neither is the same as a real survey footprint.
    \item \textbf{No chapter spans more than one redshift.} \LODI, \FOLD, and Chapter~\ref{ch:emulator} all operate at $z=0$; Chapter~\ref{ch:dps} operates at a single fixed $z=2.4$. Every result in this thesis is therefore a snapshot statement, not yet a statement about structure growth or evolution.
\end{itemize}

\section{Future work}
\label{sec:conclusion_future}

The extensions below range from a short term work, possible by a  combination of two chapters already in this thesis, to genuinely open, higher risk directions. 

\subsection{Full-box, multi-simulation DPS reconstruction}
\label{sec:conclusion_future_fullbox}

Chapter~\ref{ch:dps}'s own discussion already proposes this (Section~\ref{sec:dps_disc_future}), and it is repeated here mainly because it is the single most direct, already half built extension in the thesis. The current DPS results cover one $40\times10\times10\,(\Mpch)^3$ sub-volume, one sixteenth of the full simulation box, while \FOLD\ (Chapter~\ref{ch:cosmofold}) already solves exactly the coherent large volume generation problem this requires, via its sliding-window diffusion trajectory. Applying \FOLD's generation scheme directly to Chapter~\ref{ch:dps}'s guided reverse process, rather than treating each tile as an independent posterior draw, is the most concrete short deliverable to come out of combining two existing chapters of this thesis.

\subsection{Lightcones: stitching multi redshift emulator outputs}
\label{sec:conclusion_future_lightcone}

Every generative model in this thesis is trained and evaluated at a single, fixed redshift (coeval boxes) (Section~\ref{sec:conclusion_disc_limits}). Building a lightcone requires the opposite: a field, or a stack of fields, that varies smoothly with the observed frequency/redshift along the line of sight. The standard lightcone construction approach already used elsewhere in the field: train (or fine tune) the existing conditional architectures at a handful of discrete redshift snapshots and interpolate the conditioning between them, the same strategy used to stitch coeval boxes into 21\,cm reionization lightcones, and, more directly analogous to this thesis's own architecture, the strategy of training separate per-redshift generative models and stitching them together used for line intensity mapping lightcones by \citet[CosmoGLINT]{Moriwaki_2025}. The more ambitious direction is to make the generative model itself redshift-continuous rather than snapshot based, conditioning directly on a scalar redshift the way this thesis's models already condition on cosmological/astrophysical parameters via FiLM (Chapter~\ref{ch:emulator}) or on a coarse auxiliary field (Chapter~\ref{ch:field_pos}). Two very recent, directly relevant precedents exist for this: \citet{jacobus_lnca_2026} introduce a Lagrangian Neural Cellular Automaton that supports continuous time integration and produces full trajectories rather than discrete snapshots, explicitly framed around native lightcone construction.

\subsection{Simulation-independent (JEPA-style) representations}
\label{sec:conclusion_future_jepa}

This is a direct response to the out of distribution gap named in Section~\ref{sec:conclusion_disc_data}. Every model in this thesis has been trained and validated within one simulation suite (or, for \FOLD, one box-size regime), and Chapter~\ref{ch:emulator}'s own discussion names cross simulation suite transfer as an open, unresolved direction. \citet{skatr_ore_2025} (SKATR): a self-supervised vision transformer trained on 21\,cm maps whose learned encoding is then reused, cheaply and without retraining the encoder itself, for downstream regression and generative inference of astrophysical and cosmological parameters. Its headline result is exactly the property this thesis is missing: the authors show SKATR's representations generalize out-of-domain to differently-simulated, differently-noised, and higher-resolution datasets than the ones the encoder was trained on. This is a strikingly direct match to Chapter~\ref{ch:emulator}'s own unresolved question (transfer to an independently generated simulation, Section~\ref{sec:emu_discussion}) and to \LODI/\FOLD's 21\,cm and intensity-mapping setting specifically, more so than a generic JEPA reference would be. A complementary, CAMELS-specific precedent for the same underlying goal is \citet{jo_miest_2025}, whose Model-Insensitive ESTimator is trained across the CAMELS TNG, SIMBA, Astrid, and EAGLE simulation suites (the same suites Chapter~\ref{ch:emulator} itself draws on) to extract only features common across all four while discarding suite-specific ones, improving out-of-distribution $\Om$/$\sige$ estimation accuracy by roughly 17\%/38\% relative to a suite-specific baseline. Both approaches, and the broader self-supervised Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture family SKATR itself is built out of (\citealt{jepa_lecun_2022,jepa_assran_2023}), share the same mechanism: train an encoder to predict a representation, rather than raw pixels, of a masked or transformed input, so that simulation-suite-specific texture is discarded while the physical structure needed for the downstream task is retained, directly attacking the same failure mode already observed in Chapter~\ref{ch:cosmofold} and Chapter~\ref{ch:emulator}. Applying this to this thesis would most naturally mean pretraining a SKATR-style encoder across \FOLD's and the emulator's available simulations (CAMELS plus TNG) before the conditional generation step, rather than training the U-Net's own encoder path end-to-end on one suite as all five chapters currently do.

\subsection{Diffusion-based likelihood for simulation discrimination}
\label{sec:conclusion_future_discrimination}

The idea of using a trained diffusion model's own (approximate) likelihood or score to distinguish between candidate simulation models, rather than training a separate classifier from scratch, is a natural extension of machinery this thesis already has in hand: Chapter~\ref{ch:dps} already computes an explicit, differentiable, approximate likelihood at every reverse-diffusion step (Eq.~\ref{eq:dps_gaussian_likelihood}) as part of its guidance term. The general goal, likelihood-free discrimination between competing simulation models, is already an active line of work in a closely related setting: \citet{binnie_2025} perform simulation-based Bayesian model selection between competing reionization scenarios using a 3D CNN summary statistic inside the \texttt{pydelfi} framework, successfully distinguishing the model used to generate mock 21\,cm lightcone data in every case tested. What has not yet been done, to my knowledge, is the same kind of model discrimination using a diffusion model's own score or evidence directly, rather than a separately-trained classifier network, training one unconditional prior per candidate simulation model (or per candidate subgrid/feedback prescription) the way Chapter~\ref{ch:dps}'s prior is trained per-simulation-suite, and comparing an ELBO-based evidence estimate across priors, would be a natural, largely code-reuse extension of this thesis's own machinery into a genuinely different scientific question (model comparison rather than parameter or field inference). \emph{[Placeholder- I have not found and cannot yet verify a specific existing paper that does diffusion-likelihood-based simulation discrimination directly; the closest general-ML framing I found in searching for this (a "diffusion models as Bayesian classifiers" argument) was not precise enough to cite with confidence and is deliberately omitted here rather than guessed at.]}

\subsection{Extending DPS to survey-realistic foreground and RFI removal}
\label{sec:conclusion_future_ska}

Chapter~\ref{ch:dps}'s guided-sampling machinery is not specific to Lyman-$\alpha$ tomography; what it actually requires is an unconditional prior over the field of interest and a differentiable forward model connecting that field to a corrupted or partial observation. Radio-frequency interference flagging and foreground contamination in 21\,cm intensity mapping (Section~\ref{sec:21cm_im}) is exactly this kind of problem, with the forward model replaced by a masking/foreground-mixing operator instead of the Voigt-profile optical depth calculation. This is not a hypothetical extension: \citet{chen_chen_dvorkin_2025} already train a variational diffusion-based generative model for exactly this purpose, field-level reconstruction of 21\,cm density maps from foreground-contaminated observations, and find it achieves reconstruction quality comparable to an EFT-based field-level Bayesian method that has direct access to the true forward model, while retaining good phase information. The natural extension specific to this thesis is to train the prior on masks constructed from realistic SKA/MeerKAT flagging statistics (rather than idealized foreground contamination alone) and reuse Chapter~\ref{ch:dps}'s guidance formalism essentially unchanged, which would also be a first, concrete step toward the survey-realism gap named in Section~\ref{sec:conclusion_disc_limits}.

\subsection{Structured (large-scale-structure) initialization of the reverse diffusion process}
\label{sec:conclusion_future_lss_init}

Every diffusion model in this thesis begins its reverse process from pure Gaussian noise, $\bm{z}_T\sim\mathcal{N}(\bm 0,\bm I)$ (Section~\ref{sec:diffusion_intro}), and relies entirely on the learned network to build large-scale structure back up over many denoising steps before adding small-scale detail, a generic property of diffusion models on natural images as much as on cosmological fields. The idea of replacing that flat-spectrum Gaussian seed with a seed that already carries realistic large-scale correlations, and letting the network's job be only to add the non-linear, small-scale structure on top, has a direct precedent in \citet{randono_cloud_diffusion_2025}, who propose seeding diffusion models with scale-invariant noise explicitly motivated by the analogous role of primordial density fluctuations in structure formation. In this thesis's own setting, a cheap large-scale realization (a linear or 2LPT field, or the coarse conditioning field already used in Chapter~\ref{ch:field_pos}) is available essentially for free, so this is a case where the generic ML idea and the physical problem are unusually well matched: rather than an analogy to cosmology motivating a general noise choice, an actual cosmological field could be the seed. \citet{rouhiainen_superresolution_2024} take a closely related approach for cosmological super-resolution specifically, generating small-scale detail conditioned on (rather than seeded from) a low-resolution field via iterative outpainting, motivated by exactly the same mode-coupling argument (linear evolution at the largest scales, low-resolution $N$-body-captured non-linearity at intermediate scales, and small-scale, baryonic non-linearity left to the generative model). This is a different paper from the same first author's field-level normalizing-flow work already cited in the Introduction (\citealt{rouhiainen2021normalizingflowsrandomfields}), worth double-checking you want both, rather than conflating them, if you add this one. The concrete payoff, if this works, is a direct one: fewer reverse-diffusion steps would be needed to reach a given fidelity, since the model starts closer to the target distribution already, extending the same step-count-versus-fidelity trade-off Chapter~\ref{ch:cosmofold} already explores explicitly when reducing $T$ from 400 to 50.

\subsection{Marginalizing DPS over astrophysical and thermal-history uncertainty}
\label{sec:conclusion_future_astro}

This is not one of your original five ideas, but follows directly from the thesis-wide gap named in Section~\ref{sec:conclusion_disc_limits}: Chapter~\ref{ch:dps}'s prior is currently trained at one fixed ultraviolet background and thermal history, unlike Chapter~\ref{ch:field_pos} and Chapter~\ref{ch:emulator}, both of which already train across the CAMELS Latin Hypercube's astrophysical feedback parameters. Training Chapter~\ref{ch:dps}'s unconditional prior across a similar grid of UV background amplitude and reionization timing, rather than a single fixed model, would let the same posterior-sampling machinery marginalize over exactly the kind of astrophysical nuisance uncertainty that real Lyman-$\alpha$ forest analyses have to contend with, and would bring Chapter~\ref{ch:dps} into methodological alignment with the rest of the thesis's approach to astrophysical uncertainty. This also directly strengthens Chapter~\ref{ch:dps}'s own most novel result (Section~\ref{sec:dps_disc_dm}): a dark-matter recovery that holds up across a range of thermal histories, not only one, is a substantially stronger statement about what the learned prior has captured about structure formation generally, rather than one specific simulated Universe.

\section{Closing remark}
\label{sec:conclusion_closing}

Taken as a whole, this thesis is an argument, made empirically rather than asserted, that the field itself, not the two-point function computed from it, is where the recoverable cosmological information actually lives, and that modern generative models are now capable enough, and can be made fast enough, to work with fields directly rather than with their compressed summaries. Each chapter demonstrates one piece of the machinery this requires: painting a field, generating it coherently at scale, sampling a posterior rather than a point estimate, formalizing that posterior sampling into a general inverse-problem framework with a real physical forward model, and, finally, showing that the whole exercise pays for itself in tighter, less biased cosmological constraints. What remains, and is set out above, is mostly a matter of scale and generality rather than of new ideas: larger volumes, more redshifts, more simulation suites, more astrophysical scenarios, and more realistic data, applied to machinery that, chapter by chapter, this thesis has already shown works.
